AUTOMATION GUIDE

Invoice automation starts with the handoffs.

Invoice work is rarely just data entry. The repeat effort often sits across inboxes, PDFs, spreadsheets, accounting software, approvals and payment follow-ups. This guide shows how to identify the parts worth investigating before buying or building automation.

Where the time usually goes

Look for the full invoice journey: receiving the document, extracting fields, checking vendor details, matching purchase information, routing for approval, posting to accounting, handling exceptions and following up on missing information. The highest-value opportunity is often the handoff between two systems rather than the invoice itself.

A practical automation sequence

Start with document capture and structured extraction. Then map validation rules and approval paths. Only after those are clear should you consider posting records automatically or triggering payment workflows. Keep unusual invoices and financial exceptions under human review.

Questions to measure

How many invoices arrive each week? How much time is spent re-keying fields? How many systems receive the same information? How often do invoices need correction? How long do approvals wait? What percentage are exceptions? These measurements turn a vague automation idea into a process that can be evaluated.

What not to automate blindly

Do not remove approval controls simply because a workflow is repetitive. Financial records, supplier changes and payment instructions need appropriate verification and access controls. Automation should reduce administration without weakening financial governance.
